Doctor of Philosophy · Meteorology

Beijing, China

The modern climate represents only one realization of the diverse climate states that Earth can experience. My research explores this broader spectrum of climate states through paleoclimate and Earth system modeling.

I investigate how key components of the climate system, such as monsoons, tropical cyclones, and ocean circulation, evolve under different climate conditions, and how these changes shape the natural environment, including the cryosphere and vegetation, as well as human evolution and adaptation. More fundamentally, by extending our understanding beyond the modern observational record, I seek to uncover the physical mechanisms and interactions governing Earth's climate system, distinguish state-dependent responses from more general principles of climate dynamics, and gain insights into the possible trajectories of future climate change.

Research Interests

Climate Through Deep Time

Paleoclimate Modeling

Climate models are numerical representations of the climate system, grounded in physical laws, that allow us to simulate and understand past climate states.

Hydrological Cycle

Monsoons and tropical cyclones are major precipitation systems; understanding how their rainfall and precipitation variability change across climate states is a central scientific question.

Cryosphere

Glacier-climate interactions reveal how changes in temperature and precipitation shape glacier extent, mass balance, and equilibrium line altitude.

Multiscale Variability

The climate system responds to different external forcings across timescales, from tectonic and orbital changes to millennial variability, through distinct physical mechanisms and feedbacks.
